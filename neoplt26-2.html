    Second-line therapy — IVIG / corticosteroids (immune-mediated: ITP or NAIT)

    Shown because immune-mediated thrombocytopenia (maternal ITP or NAIT) risk factors are present. Neonatal-specific trial evidence for IVIG/steroid dosing is limited — most figures below are extrapolated from older-child ITP practice. Confirm every dose with pediatric hematology and pharmacy before prescribing; this box does not order or administer anything.

    IVIG

    • Commonly cited regimen: 1 g/kg as a single infusion; may repeat once after 24–48 hours if the platelet response is inadequate.
    • Alternative regimen sometimes used: 0.4 g/kg/day for 3–5 days.
    • Response is typically seen at 24–72 hours — let the count response guide whether to repeat, rather than a fixed schedule.
    • Confirm brand-specific infusion rate, sodium/sugar content (renal/cardiac status), and premedication policy with pharmacy — these vary by product and are not standardised here.

    Corticosteroids

    • IV methylprednisolone is the option more commonly used in neonatal practice: typically cited around 1–2 mg/kg/day divided every 12 hours, short course (roughly 3–7 days), tapered if given longer than a few days.
    • Dexamethasone pulses are used more often in older children/adults with ITP than in neonates; neonatal evidence is very limited, so treat it as a specialist-selected alternative rather than a routine neonatal first choice.
    • The choice between the two, and the exact dose/duration, should follow your pediatric hematology protocol — this reflects a widely-cited practice pattern, not a validated neonatal dosing standard.
    ⚠️ Discharge & follow-up safety points

    Applies whenever the infant goes home with a low or recovering platelet count, and/or after IVIG or corticosteroid therapy. Give this in writing to the family with an emergency contact number.

    When to seek urgent medical attention
    • New or spreading bruising/petechiae, or any fresh bleeding (gums, nose, blood in stool/vomit/urine)
    • Unusual lethargy, poor feeding, high-pitched cry, irritability, or a bulging fontanelle (possible intracranial bleeding)
    • Fever, or any deterioration the family is worried about
    Injections while the count is low
    • Avoid intramuscular injections (including IM vaccines) while the platelet count remains significantly low — use the subcutaneous route or defer per local policy, and confirm with the team before any IM dose (e.g. vitamin K if not already given)
    • Apply firm, prolonged pressure after any heel-prick or venepuncture
    Repeat count in follow-up clinic
    • Typically recheck CBC/platelet count within 48–72 hours of discharge, or sooner if instructed by the treating team
    • Continue serial counts until a clear, sustained rising trend or normalisation is confirmed
    Escalate to pediatric hematology follow-up
    • Count has not normalised in the expected timeframe, or is falling again after initial response
    • Confirmed or strongly suspected NAIT/ITP requiring structured monitoring, or recurrent/unexplained thrombocytopenia
    • Any bleeding recurrence, or poor/no response to treatment given
    Vaccination
    • If IVIG was given: defer live-attenuated vaccines (MMR, varicella, rotavirus, live-attenuated influenza) — commonly cited intervals are on the order of 8–11 months depending on dose; confirm the exact interval against your national immunisation schedule
    • Routine inactivated vaccines can generally proceed on schedule unless the count remains very low — discuss timing/route with primary care
    • Document the deferral clearly for the family and primary care/GP
    Future pregnancy notes (if NAIT / maternal ITP)
    • Refer the mother for hematology/obstetric counselling before any future pregnancy
    • Document the infant's diagnosis, any HPA typing/antibody results, and treatment given — this informs antenatal risk assessment and monitoring next time
    • Future pregnancies may need specialist antenatal planning (e.g. serial fetal monitoring or antenatal IVIG) per hematology/obstetric input
